# Style Bible

## Tone

Precise, warm, restrained, intelligent.

The film should feel like a meeting point between:

- a scientific diagram;
- a hand-drawn animation;
- a systems model;
- a children's picture book for adults.

Avoid sentimentality without becoming cold.

## Palette

- background: near-black;
- structure/text: warm off-white;
- inactive/weak links: graphite/grey;
- active connection: one warm signal colour;
- scars/damage: muted contrasting tone if needed.

The signal colour should be rare enough that its appearance means something.

## People

People are represented primarily as points, circles, traces, or minimal abstract forms.

Avoid generic illustrated characters unless a later scene genuinely needs them.

## The thread

One continuous thread is the central motif.

It can become:

- path;
- bridge;
- waveform;
- line of communication;
- crack repair;
- network edge;
- family relation;
- structural support.

It should always feel like the same underlying visual object.

## Typography

Lyrics are part of the scene, not subtitles laid on top.

Examples:

- **SHARE** can split into two readable halves;
- **LISTEN** can receive an incoming waveform;
- **WRONG** can trigger a coordinate correction;
- **FORGIVE** can reconnect after a visible break;
- **APART** can literally separate;
- **TOGETHER** can assemble;
- **POSSIBLE** can change from a dotted potential edge into a solid connection.

## Avoid

- hearts;
- halos;
- glowing hands;
- stock footage of smiling people;
- sentimental family montage;
- generic AI imagery;
- neon cyberpunk;
- particle nebulae;
- visual effects without semantic purpose.

Beauty should come from structure, timing, and discovery.
